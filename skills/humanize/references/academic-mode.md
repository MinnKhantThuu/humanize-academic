# Academic Mode — assignments, essays, coursework

Activate when the task is an essay, assignment, report, literature review,
reflection, or any assessed academic writing. Formal register stays; robotic
rhythm goes.

## Assignment types and what to preserve

| Type | Preserve absolutely | Humanizing freedom |
|---|---|---|
| Analytical essay | thesis, argument order, evidence-to-analysis pairing | sentence rhythm, word choice, transitions |
| Reflective essay | the events and lessons described | first person, uncertainty, mixed feelings |
| Literature review | every source claim and its citation | synthesis phrasing, paragraph grouping |
| Lab/project report | data, methods, results | discussion voice, limitation phrasing |
| Application/cover letter | facts, roles, dates | motivation phrasing, tone |

## Citations — untouchable tokens

An in-text citation is part of the sentence's furniture, not its prose.

**Rules:**
1. Never remove, move across sentences, or reformat a citation.
2. Preserve the citation style the input uses, exactly:
   - APA: `(Smith, 2020)` / `(Smith, 2020, p. 3)` / Smith (2020) argues...
   - MLA: `(Smith 12)` / Smith argues... (12)
   - Harvard: `(Smith 2020)` / `(Smith 2020, p. 3)`
   - Footnote markers `[1]`, `¹` stay attached to their sentence.
3. When paraphrasing a sentence that carries a citation, the citation must
   remain on the paraphrased sentence that makes the same claim. If the
   rewrite merges two cited claims into one sentence, both citations stay.
4. Never edit anything inside quotation marks. If a quotation is embedded,
   keep it verbatim with its citation; you may change only the framing
   words around it ("Smith argues that" → "For Smith, the finding was
   clear:" — the quote itself does not move or change).
5. The reference list / bibliography / works cited is not prose. Never
   rewrite, reorder, or reformat it. Skip it entirely.
6. If a claim in the input has no citation, do not add one. If the rewrite
   makes a claim vaguer than the cited original, that is an error.

## Academic hedging — the human version

Delete the template hedges, keep honest uncertainty:

| AI hedge (delete) | Human academic hedge (use when genuinely uncertain) |
|---|---|
| "It is important to note that..." | (state the thing directly) |
| "It could be argued that..." | "The evidence suggests..." / "One reading is..." |
| "This demonstrates the importance of..." | "This points to..." / "Which may explain..." |
| "plays a crucial role in" | "drives" / "shapes" / "is central to" |
| "significantly impacts" | "changes" / "reduces" / "raises" (be specific) |
| "Further research is needed" (as a filler closer) | Only when the input actually names a gap |

## Vocabulary — the band 7–8 ceiling

Allowed and encouraged: precise academic verbs and nouns a strong student
uses — "demonstrates", "indicates", "suggests", "contradicts", "undermines",
"accounts for", "consistent with", "whereas", "by contrast", "implication",
"limitation", "finding", "framework".

Avoid (band-9 flex words that read as AI or as ghostwriting): "perfunctory",
"obfuscate", "recalcitrant", "epochal", "quintessential", "verisimilitude",
"ineluctable". Rule of thumb: if the word would look strange next to the
writer's other sentences, choose the simpler precise one.

Sentence connectives that read human in academic prose: "Yet", "Even so",
"That said", "The harder question is", "This matters because", "By
contrast", "Which raises", "The catch is that". Use each at most once per
essay.

## Rhythm inside formal register

Burstiness rules apply to academic writing too, tuned down:

- Target sentence-length variance ≥ 40% of the mean sentence length.
- One short sentence (≤ 8 words) per 200 words is enough in formal text.
- Break thesis-sentence patterns: not every paragraph opens with "X is Y".
  Vary openers: a fact, a contrast, a citation-led sentence, a question
  (sparingly, at most one per essay), a subordinate clause.
- Paragraphs of 3–6 sentences; one paragraph of two sentences is fine as a
  hinge; no one-sentence "mic drop" closers.

## The consistency trap (what actually gets caught)

Graders compare one piece against the writer's other work and in-class
performance. The rewrite must not introduce:
- Vocabulary dramatically above the writer's demonstrated level.
- A structure unlike the assignment prompt's required structure.
- Personal experience the writer cannot claim (never invent fieldwork,
  lab sessions, or reading the input does not mention).
- Perfect uniform paragraph lengths (a real tell; vary 3–7 sentences).

## Word count discipline

- Keep the final within ±10% of the input unless told otherwise.
- If the input is under the required count, do not pad with restatement;
  report the shortfall to the user and suggest where they could add
  analysis or a source-based example.
- If over, trim the weakest restatement, never evidence or citation.

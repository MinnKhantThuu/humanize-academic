# Self-audit checklist — run on every draft (pass 3)

Answer each honestly. Any "fail" means fix the draft before finalizing.

## 1. Perplexity
- [ ] Does at least one word choice per paragraph surprise without straining?
- [ ] Are all banned-vocabulary words gone (delve, pivotal, tapestry,
      moreover, furthermore, in conclusion, "It is important to note"...)? 

## 2. Burstiness
- [ ] Is there a sentence of ≤ 6 words (≤ 8 in academic mode) per 150 words?
- [ ] Are there three consecutive sentences within five words of the same
      length? (If yes: break one up or merge two.)

## 3. Hedges
- [ ] Are template hedges gone ("it is important to note", "generally
      speaking", "could be argued" with no arguer)?
- [ ] Do remaining hedges express real uncertainty from the source?

## 4. Structure
- [ ] Does any paragraph restate its opening at the end? (Cut it.)
- [ ] Is there an "In conclusion" or summary paragraph that only repeats?
- [ ] Do bullet/numbered structures exist because the content needs them,
      or because a template put them there?

## 5. Specificity
- [ ] Does every abstract claim have its grounding anchor from the source
      (number, name, date, example)?
- [ ] Was anything specific invented that the input does not contain? (Fail:
      remove it.)

## 6. Voice
- [ ] Did assistant openers/closers survive? ("Great question", "I hope
      this helps", "Would you like...")
- [ ] Does the register match the text type (reflective = personal allowed;
      analytical = perspective through choice, not "I")?
- [ ] Are there unprompted balanced tradeoffs ("While X has merits, Y also
      ...") that the argument does not need?

## 7. Transitions
- [ ] Is every "Furthermore/Moreover/Additionally" gone or replaced with a
      connective that actually connects?
- [ ] Do at least two adjacent paragraphs rely on content logic rather than
      a transition word?

## 8. Punctuation
- [ ] Em dash count ≤ 1 per 300 words (and only if input used dashes)?
- [ ] Semicolons limited to comma-containing lists?
- [ ] Any mid-sentence colon left? ("The problem: ...")
- [ ] Quotes straightened?

## 9. Citations and facts (academic mode)
- [ ] Every citation still attached to the sentence that makes its claim,
      in the original format?
- [ ] Quotation marks contents byte-identical to the input?
- [ ] Reference list untouched?
- [ ] Word count within ±10% of input?

## Final read
- [ ] Read the whole thing aloud in your head. If any sentence sounds like
      an announcement ("That is the real win.") or a template ("As we can
      see from the above discussion..."), rewrite that sentence.
- [ ] Would this survive a grader comparing it with the writer's other
      work? (Level consistency, no invented experience.)
